Most industrial chemical routes were designed when energy was cheap and a
separate unit for every task was the obvious answer. Combining reaction and
separation in one vessel can cut both capital and utility cost substantially —
but it also couples the design variables, so the configuration that works can
no longer be found by intuition. It has to be searched for.

This direction does that search for chemical intermediates.

### What we work on

- **Reactive distillation and side-stream configurations** for equilibrium- and
  selectivity-limited reactions.
- **Dividing-wall columns** and other heat-integrated separation schemes.
- **Design and control together** — an intensified column that saves 20% energy
  and cannot be controlled is not a saving. Controllability is assessed as part
  of the design, not after it.
- **Downstream processing of Indonesian commodities** — routes that convert
  local feedstocks into higher-value intermediates instead of exporting them
  raw.

### Where it stands

A published study on energy-saving design and control of a side-streams
reactive distillation configuration for diphenyl carbonate production anchors
this direction; see [Publications](/publications/). Work on Indonesian
commodity routes is at the scoping stage.

### What a student would do here

Steady-state design first, then dynamic simulation and a control structure that
survives realistic disturbances. Plan for the second half of the work to be
harder than the first.
