Indonesia's aviation and heavy-transport sectors will need drop-in fuels long
before they can be electrified. The chemistry to make them from waste oils is
established; what is not settled is **which process configuration is worth
building**. Yield, hydrogen consumption, energy demand, capital cost, and
carbon intensity pull against each other, and a configuration that wins on one
loses on another.

This direction designs and screens those configurations.

### What we work on

- **Hydroprocessing of various lipid feedstock** into renewable diesel and
  sustainable aviation fuel — reaction pathway selection, reactor
  configuration, and separation train design.
- **Non-adiabatic and intensified reactor designs** for the
  hydrotreating–hydrocracking–isomerization sequence, where heat management
  drives both selectivity and utility cost.
- **Electro-fuels (e-SAF)** — process design and techno-economic evaluation of
  routes driven by renewable electricity and captured carbon.


### Where it stands

Two published studies underpin this direction: a multi-objective assessment of
intensified non-adiabatic reactor cooling configurations for waste-cooking-oil
hydroprocessing, and an integrated process design and techno-economic
evaluation of e-SAF. Both are on the [Publications](/publications/) page.

### What a student would do here

Build the flowsheet in Aspen Plus, get it to converge reliably, then hand it to
an optimizer and defend the trade-off you chose. Expect to spend more time on
convergence and on justifying your cost basis than on drawing the flowsheet.
